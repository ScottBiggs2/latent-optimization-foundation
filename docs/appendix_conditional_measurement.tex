\section{Measuring conditional generation in weight space}
\label{app:conditional-measurement}

This appendix defines the instrument used to decide whether a generative model over
neural network weights is actually \emph{conditioned} on its input, as distinct from
merely producing plausible weights. The distinction is not academic: on a
mean-centred ensemble the two are easily confused, and the statistic most naturally
reached for reports a correctly-conditioned model as a failure.

\subsection{Setup and notation}
\label{app:setup}

Let $\mathcal{Z} = \{w_1,\dots,w_N\}$ be a zoo of $N$ language models sharing one
architecture, each flattened to a vector $w_i \in \mathbb{R}^{D}$. Each member is
associated with a \emph{pretraining data mixture} $\pi_i \in \Delta^{P-1}$, a point on
the simplex over $P$ text domains. In the runs reported here the architecture is
GPT-2 \cite{radford2019gpt2} at a reduced scale, $N = 100$, $D = 51{,}475{,}968$
(embeddings and biases included), and $P = 5$.

All members descend from one initialisation and one shared partially-trained trunk,
diverging only over the final fraction of training. Members sharing an initialisation
and a burn-in remain linearly mode connected \cite{frankle2020lmc}, so permutation
symmetry is broken by construction and no weight-space canonicalisation is applied.
The zoo is a local manifold around one trunk, not a sample of the global weight
distribution.

The zoo is \emph{nested}: $20$ members are \emph{anchors}, comprising $5$ one-hot
mixtures replicated across $4$ branches each, and $80$ are \emph{singletons} drawn
from $\mathrm{Dirichlet}(\alpha{=}1)$, giving $85$ distinct mixtures. The anchor
replicates are load-bearing for everything below: they are the only source of
variation \emph{at fixed $\pi$}.

A dual (Gram) PCA over the $N$ members yields codes $c_i \in \mathbb{R}^{k}$ with
$k \le N-1$. Codes are then affinely normalised so that the training set has unit
root-mean-square,
%
\begin{equation}
x_i \;=\; \frac{c_i - \bar{c}}{s},
\qquad
s \;=\; \sqrt{\tfrac{1}{k}\textstyle\sum_j \mathrm{Var}_i(c_{ij})},
\qquad
\mathrm{RMS}(X) \approx 1 .
\end{equation}
%
The scale $s$ is a single scalar rather than a per-dimension whitening, so PCA's
variance ordering survives normalisation. All dispersion statistics below are
computed in this normalised code space.

\subsection{The conditional flow}
\label{app:flow}

The generator is a conditional rectified flow \cite{liu2022rectified,lipman2022flow}.
With source $x_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$, data point $x_1$, and $t \sim U(0,1)$,
%
\begin{equation}
x_t = (1-t)\,x_0 + t\,x_1,
\qquad
u = x_1 - x_0,
\qquad
\mathcal{L} = \mathbb{E}\big\|\,v_\theta(x_t, t, \mathbf{c}) - u \,\big\|^2 ,
\end{equation}
%
integrated with explicit Euler. The conditioning representation
$\mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_c}$ is produced by one of two encoders:
%
\begin{align}
\textbf{discrete:}\quad & \mathbf{c} = \phi\big(E[\,f_i\,]\big),
  && E \in \mathbb{R}^{F \times d_c} \ \text{an embedding table, } f_i \ \text{a family index},\\
\textbf{mixture:}\quad  & \mathbf{c} = \phi\big(\mathrm{MLP}(\pi_i)\big),
  && \mathrm{MLP}: \mathbb{R}^{P} \to \mathbb{R}^{d_c} \ \text{two layers, SiLU},
\end{align}
%
where $\phi$ is a shared projection. The encoder-MLP pattern is
FiLM's \cite{perez2018film}; diffusion transformers use the same shape for their
timestep and label embedders \cite{peebles2023dit}. Classifier-free
guidance \cite{ho2022cfg} is applied to the velocity field,
$v = v_\varnothing + \gamma\,(v_{\mathbf{c}} - v_\varnothing)$, with a learned null
token $v_\varnothing$ and conditioning dropout at training time. Because guidance acts
on the post-projection $\mathbf{c}$ and never on the label, it transfers unchanged from
the discrete to the continuous encoder.

\paragraph{The discrete encoder is the control, not a legacy path.}
In a single-architecture zoo every member shares one family index, so the discrete
encoder is \emph{constant} and the flow is unconditional. The two encoders therefore
form an exact ablation at fixed codes, fixed network size and fixed training budget.

\subsection{Why the natural metrics are insufficient}
\label{app:why}

\paragraph{Held-out perplexity cannot police a generative arm.}
The ensemble is mean-centred and, for a zoo branched from a shared trunk, the mean is
itself approximately a good model. A generator that collapses toward the mean
therefore attains a \emph{better} change in perplexity than an honest sample while
generating nothing. We previously measured a collapsed flow beating a Gaussian null
by $43\times$ on $\Delta$PPL while emitting at $0.25\times$ the correct dispersion. A
Gaussian null cannot detect this, because a collapsed generator beats it by
construction.

\paragraph{Marginal dispersion detects collapse but not conditioning.}
The natural repair is to measure spread directly,
%
\begin{equation}
r_{\mathrm{pooled}} \;=\;
\frac{\mathrm{RMS}\big(\,\text{generated codes}\,\big)}{\mathrm{RMS}\big(\,\text{real codes}\,\big)} ,
\label{eq:pooled}
\end{equation}
%
which reads $1$ for a faithful generator and falls toward $0$ under collapse. It is
necessary and it is not sufficient: \textbf{a generator that ignores its conditioning
entirely still emits the correct total spread and scores a perfect $1.0$.}

\paragraph{Worse, \eqref{eq:pooled} inverts under conditioning.}
Samples drawn at a \emph{fixed} $\pi$ carry the conditional spread, whereas the
denominator of \eqref{eq:pooled} carries the pooled spread, which also contains all
between-mixture variation. Writing $\rho = \mathrm{RMS}_{\text{within}} /
\mathrm{RMS}_{\text{pooled}}$, a \emph{perfectly} conditioned generator evaluated this
way reads $r_{\mathrm{pooled}} = \rho$. Measured here $\rho \approx 0.33$ --- which is
numerically indistinguishable from the $0.25$ recorded for genuine collapse. Reported
naively, a correct model and a catastrophic one are the same number.

\subsection{A conditional dispersion statistic}
\label{app:atpi}

The anchor replicates supply the missing reference. The four branches of an anchor
were trained on an \emph{identical} mixture and differ only through data order and
initialisation of the branch, so their spread is the irreducible noise of ``train a
model on this mixture'' --- precisely the width a correct conditional distribution
should have. Let $X_a$ be the codes of one anchor group and $S$ be $n$ samples drawn
from the generator at that anchor's mixture. Define
%
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{RMS}_{\text{within}} = \mathrm{RMS}\big(X_a - \overline{X_a}\big),
\qquad
a_\pi \;=\; \frac{\mathrm{RMS}\big(S - \overline{S}\big)}{\mathrm{RMS}_{\text{within}}} .
\label{eq:atpi}
\end{equation}
%
Three landmarks make $a_\pi$ readable:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
$a_\pi \approx 0$   & point mass per mixture --- memorisation or collapse \\
$a_\pi \approx 1$   & correct conditional width \\
$a_\pi \approx 1/\rho$ & the full marginal spread --- \textbf{the conditioning was ignored} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{Recovery: a rank-invariant summary}
\label{app:recovery}

$a_\pi$ is awkward to compare across ranks, because truncating the code space
discards within-mixture noise and so shrinks its denominator: $\rho$ falls from
$0.320$ at $k{=}99$ to $0.022$ at $k{=}10$, and a raw $a_\pi$ of $23$ at low rank
reflects that shrinkage rather than a defective model. The law of total variance
gives a scale-free alternative. Decomposing
%
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Var}_{\text{total}}
= \underbrace{\mathbb{E}_\pi\big[\mathrm{Var}(x \mid \pi)\big]}_{\text{within}}
+ \underbrace{\mathrm{Var}_\pi\big[\mathbb{E}(x \mid \pi)\big]}_{\text{between}} ,
\end{equation}
%
the fraction of code variance attributable to the mixture is
$1 - \mathbb{E}[\mathrm{Var}(x\mid\pi)]/\mathrm{Var}_{\text{total}}$. Estimating the
conditional variance from the generator and the total from the data,
%
\begin{align}
\text{explained}_{\text{flow}} &= 1 - \big(a_\pi \cdot \rho\big)^2, \label{eq:flowvar}\\
\text{ceiling}_{\text{data}}   &= 1 - \rho^2, \label{eq:ceiling}\\
\textbf{recovery} &= \text{explained}_{\text{flow}} \,/\, \text{ceiling}_{\text{data}} .
\label{eq:recovery}
\end{align}
%
Recovery is $0$ when the mixture is ignored and $1$ when the generator reproduces all
of the conditional structure the data contains at that rank. Equation
\eqref{eq:ceiling} must be evaluated \emph{per rank}: truncation mechanically raises
the share the mixture could explain, and comparing a low-rank model against a
high-rank ceiling would overstate it.

\paragraph{Why recovery $\approx 0$ is a conclusion, not a coincidence.}
One might object that matching widths does not preclude the conditional \emph{mean}
moving with $\pi$. It does. If $\mathbb{E}(x \mid \pi)$ varied appreciably while
$\mathrm{Var}(x \mid \pi)$ remained at the marginal variance, the total variance would
strictly exceed the marginal --- a contradiction. Measuring the two equal forces the
between-mixture term to vanish.

\subsection{A pairing control}
\label{app:pairing}

A generative null result and a corrupted data join are indistinguishable after the
fact: if codes are paired with the wrong mixtures, the model trains on shuffled
conditioning and every downstream conditioning statistic is flat for a reason that
has nothing to do with modelling. We therefore report
%
\begin{equation}
\tau \;=\; \mathrm{corr}_{i<j}\Big(\; \|\pi_i - \pi_j\|_1 \;,\; \|x_i - x_j\|_2 \;\Big),
\end{equation}
%
computed over all member pairs. No generative model is involved: $\tau$ is a property
of the data, it is positive when mixture distance predicts weight-space distance, and
it is $\approx 0$ under a shuffled join. Measured $\tau \in [{+}0.63, {+}0.69]$ across
all reported cells, against $O(10^{-2})$ for a permuted control.

\subsection{Results}
\label{app:results}

\paragraph{The conditioning axis is inert at the default training budget.}
Across $16$ cells spanning two divergence settings $\beta \in \{0.15, 0.30\}$, two
basis constructions, two ranks and both encoders, $r_{\mathrm{pooled}}$ lies in
$[0.85, 1.10]$ --- nothing is collapsed --- and $\tau \in [{+}0.63, {+}0.69]$. Yet
recovery is $\approx 2\%$ at $k = N{-}1$ and $\approx 20\%$ at $k = N/2$. The marginal
statistic \eqref{eq:pooled} reads healthy in \emph{every} one of these cells.

\paragraph{The cause is optimisation, not expressive capacity.}
Table~\ref{tab:diagnosis} separates the candidates. The dataset has $85$ rows and the
batch size is $64$, so the default $500$ epochs is roughly $10^3$ optimiser steps.

Three observations follow.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The conditioning encoder was never the constraint.} It is a two-layer
MLP on a $5$-dimensional input, and the structure to be represented is at most
$\dim(\Delta^{4}) = 4$-dimensional. Holding it fixed and increasing the training
budget alone moves recovery from $27.6\%$ to $90.2\%$.

\item \textbf{Rank is a convergence-rate axis, not a structural one.} At $k = N-1$
recovery appears hopeless at $1.8\%$, but reaches $74.6\%$ with additional capacity
and duration. Lower ranks converge faster because truncation removes noise
directions; they do not unlock structure that $k = N-1$ cannot reach.

\item \textbf{Over-fitting is present, but it is invisible to the marginal
statistic.} At $20{,}000$ epochs $a_\pi$ falls to $0.955$: the generator's conditional
distribution is now \emph{narrower} than genuine within-mixture variation, the
signature of memorising the training points at each mixture. Simultaneously
$r_{\mathrm{pooled}} = 1.081$, entirely healthy. On a flat, unstructured ensemble the
same over-fitting instead collapses the \emph{marginal}, where \eqref{eq:pooled}
detects it readily. The failure mode does not disappear with structured data; it
migrates to a statistic that is not routinely reported.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The retrieval baseline}
\label{app:retrieval}

Dispersion statistics constrain the \emph{shape} of the generated distribution and say
nothing about whether generation beats lookup. For a held-out mixture $\pi$ we compare
the generated model against the trained model whose mixture is nearest to $\pi$ in
$L^1$ on the simplex. Models are scored by the mixture-weighted mean log perplexity,
%
\begin{equation}
L(m, \pi) \;=\; \sum_{d=1}^{P} \pi_d \, \log \mathrm{ppl}_d(m) ,
\end{equation}
%
which weights each domain by the mixture the model was asked to embody; the logarithm
makes the quantity additive, since perplexity composes multiplicatively. The anchor
replicates again supply the resolution: $\sigma$, the standard deviation of $L$ across
an anchor's four branches, is the noise floor of the instrument, measured
$\sigma = 0.0076$ nats at $\beta = 0.15$.

Define the \emph{headroom} $H = L(\text{retrieval}, \pi) - L(\text{ceiling}, \pi)$,
where the ceiling is the model actually trained at $\pi$. $H$ is the margin any
generator has available. We pre-register the rule that a recovery fraction
$(L_{\text{retr}} - L_{\text{gen}})/H$ is reported only where $H > 3\sigma$;
otherwise the cell is declared unresolvable and only the raw difference with its error
bar is given. Without this guard the ratio diverges with arbitrary sign on precisely
the cells that carry no information.

Table~\ref{tab:retrieval} reports a design consequence worth stating explicitly. The
interior held-out mixtures are, by construction, those nearest the barycentre of the
simplex --- the densest region of the sample. Retrieval is consequently at or better
than the ceiling in three of four cells, and \textbf{no generative claim is available
in them at all}. Only the held-out vertex, an extrapolation target, leaves headroom.
A generative result quoted on interior interpolation alone would be unfalsifiable.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{app:limitations}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{No dispersion statistic can establish generalisation.} A value
$a_\pi \approx 1$ is equally consistent with having learned $p(x \mid \pi)$ and with
having memorised the four anchor members at that mixture: a mixture of four memorised
point masses has exactly the correct conditional spread. Separating these requires a
held-out mixture evaluated against the retrieval baseline of
\S\ref{app:retrieval}. This is the same concern that motivates nearest-neighbour
retrieval baselines in the diffusion memorisation
literature \cite{carlini2023extracting,somepalli2023replication}.

\item \textbf{The within-mixture reference is a four-sample estimate}, drawn from
anchor groups that sit at simplex vertices and are therefore the most extreme
mixtures present. The rank- and duration-dependence reported in
Table~\ref{tab:diagnosis} is consistent across many independent cells and is robust;
the absolute scale of $\rho$ should not be quoted to three significant figures. The
$k = 25$ and $k = 10$ rows in particular have denominators small enough
($\rho = 0.031$, $0.022$) to be noise-dominated, and are reported as directional only.

\item \textbf{Conditioning on a near-unique key.} With $85$ distinct mixtures over
$100$ members, $80$ of them observed exactly once, there is little or no replication
at most conditioning values. This is the regime in which continuous-conditional
generative models are known to be difficult to estimate \cite{ding2021ccgan}, and it
is a property of the zoo's construction rather than of the model.

\item \textbf{All statistics in \S\ref{app:atpi}--\S\ref{app:pairing} are computed in
code space.} They constrain the geometry of the generated distribution and do not
establish that a generated model is functionally good. Functional evaluation is
reported separately.
\end{enumerate}
